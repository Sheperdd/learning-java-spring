# The container vs `new`: what M3 actually shows

Shane asked "why beans, and how is this different from just using `new`?" after writing `AppConfig`. He had read `@Bean` as extra ceremony around `new`. That's a fair reading at three classes, and it was corrected to "the container runs `new` for you and manages the whole object graph". Asked which payoffs M3 already demonstrates, he named exactly **ordering** and **sharing**. He did not claim config-driven choice, lifecycle or wrapping, which are still to come. Earlier, he explained a second repository as "you wouldn't know which repo your account is in" (plus memory). The sharper point, split state (balances silently disagreeing between services), was supplied to him.

**Evidence:** the 01 M3 discussion. He wrote `AppConfig` with parameter-style `@Bean` methods; his first attempt mixed method calls into the parameter list, and he fixed it after one hint. `SpringWiredApp` uses `getBean(Type.class)`. He suggested lookup by name first.
**Implications:** M4 should pay off the "promises": `@ConditionalOnProperty` (choice from config) and `@SpringBootApplication`'s scanning. Ask him to name, before showing, what Boot removes compared with `AppConfig`. Not yet shown: the CGLIB `getClass()` experiment, the prototype-scope "make the test fail" experiment, and `isSameAs` vs `isEqualTo` (he had Claude write `AppConfigTest`). Revisit these in M5 (scopes). Try-with-resources was new to him in this session; check it when lifecycle comes up.
